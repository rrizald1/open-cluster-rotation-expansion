About this study

Open clusters evolve dynamically under the influence of internal processes and the galactic tidal field, producing signatures such as rotation, expansion, and extended tidal tails. While rotation has been studied mainly in cluster cores, recent observations and N-body simulations suggest that kinematic signatures may extend into the tidal tails and that the rotation axis may change as stars escape from the cluster.

This study investigates the signatures of rotation and expansion from cluster cores to tidal tails using the open cluster sample and tidal-tail membership identified by Risbud et al. (2025). The goal is to explore how these kinematic signatures vary between the core and tails and provide observational insight into the dynamical evolution of open clusters.

How to read these plots
Core+Tails / Core-only / Tails-only
The three membership samples tested in parallel for every cluster — combined, core stars only, and tidal-tail stars only.
Rotation — Radial Velocity
The rotation axis is identified from the PA with the maximum velocity difference between opposite sides. The rotation component is then obtained from the RVcorr–XR slope, where XR is the perpendicular distance from the rotation axis.
Rotation — Proper Motion
Tangential proper motion plotted against distance from the cluster center; a significant slope indicates rotation.
Expansion / contraction
Radial proper motion plotted against distance from the center; a positive slope indicates expansion, a negative slope indicates contraction.
